NIGERIAN EXCHANGE (NGX) FINANCIAL ANALYSIS
Month in Review: August 2026
Prepared: September 3, 2026
================================================================

1. HEADLINE SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Nigerian equities closed August 2026 on a mixed note. The
benchmark NGX All-Share Index (ASI) declined -0.44% for the
month, snapping the market's run of consecutive monthly gains,
even though a strong final trading day (+1.20%, adding N1.91
trillion) helped claw back much of the month's losses. Despite
the pullback, the market retained the bulk of its exceptional
2026 rally.

2. THE MONTH IN SHAPE
----------------------------------------------------------------
- The market endured an 11-session losing streak during August,
  dragging the ASI down to a monthly low of 238,682.92 points
  on August 26.
- Confirmation that Nigeria will return to FTSE Russell's
  Frontier Market status (effective September 21, 2026) helped
  snap the losing streak.
- August 31 alone contributed a +1.20% single-day gain, adding
  N1.91 trillion in market cap (from N155.83tn to N157.74tn),
  on trading value of N38.66 billion (606.13 million shares,
  53,364 deals) with breadth of 43 advancers vs 17 decliners
  (2.53x advance/decline ratio).
- Full-month market breadth: 43 gainers vs 17 losers on the
  final session, indicative of broad-based buying interest into
  month-end despite the overall monthly decline.

Reading: August was less a broad sell-off than a sharp rotation.
Money moved into banking, consumer goods, and value/dividend
names, and out of insurance, industrial goods, and richly-valued
growth stocks.

Banking sector detail: The NGX Banking Index closed the first
week of August at 2,526.57 points (+68% YTD at that point) and
ended the month at 2,624.05 points, the strongest-performing
major sector for August. Gains were led by First HoldCo, Access
Holdings, Zenith Bank, GTCO, Stanbic IBTC, and Ecobank as
investors repositioned ahead of dividend season and on continued
confidence in the post-recapitalisation banking landscape.

6. SECTOR NARRATIVES
----------------------------------------------------------------
Banking: Clear winner. Access Holdings and First HoldCo led
selective gains; the sector benefited from post-recapitalisation
balance sheet strength and dividend-season positioning.

Consumer Goods: Sharply mixed. Unilever Nigeria, International
Breweries, BUA Foods, and Dangote Sugar posted sizeable declines,
while Vitafoam, Nigerian Breweries, NASCON, and Guinness Nigeria
advanced — the sector index still finished positive overall
(+3.47%) on strength elsewhere in the basket.

Oil & Gas: A similarly split story. Seplat Energy and Eterna
gained sharply while Aradel Holdings, Oando, TotalEnergies, and
Japaul Gold declined — the sector index closed essentially flat
(+0.25%).

Insurance: The month's clear laggard (-9.26%), continuing a
pattern of underperformance versus banking despite occasional
single-name rallies (e.g., International Energy Insurance had
spiked earlier in the year before giving back gains in August).

Industrial Goods: Declined -2.03%, with several heavyweight names
(Dangote Cement, Berger Paints, Beta Glass, Geregu Power,
Transcorp Power) closing August unchanged from July 31 levels —
suggesting limited institutional repositioning in this sector
during the month.

7. MACRO / STRUCTURAL CONTEXT
----------------------------------------------------------------
- FTSE Russell Frontier Market reclassification: Confirmed
  effective September 21, 2026, following an assessment of
  Nigeria's market infrastructure and T+1 settlement framework.
  This is widely cited by market participants as a catalyst for
  improved international visibility and potential fresh foreign
  portfolio inflows.
- Investor composition: Domestic retail and institutional
  investors remained dominant, accounting for roughly 90% of
  transacted value in August; foreign portfolio participation
  stayed comparatively limited.
- H1 2026 corporate fundamentals: Nigeria's ten leading NGX
  blue-chip companies collectively generated N14.4 trillion in
  revenue in H1 2026 despite elevated inflation, high borrowing
  costs, and currency volatility — underpinning the broader
  bullish narrative for the year even amid August's pullback.
- Naira: Reported to have strengthened past the N1,330/$ mark
  during the period, its strongest level in roughly two years,
  which analysts linked to improving investor sentiment.

9. OUTLOOK GOING INTO SEPTEMBER 2026
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Analysts (e.g., David Adonri, Highcap Securities) expect the
  market to sustain positive momentum into September, with
  investors likely to stay focused on fundamentally strong,
  relatively undervalued stocks rather than chasing growth names.
- The FTSE Russell Frontier Market effective date (Sept 21, 2026)
  is the single largest near-term catalyst being watched.
- The broader theme heading into September: investors are
  becoming more selective, rotating toward value and established
  earnings and away from expensive growth and structurally
  weaker sectors (notably insurance).
